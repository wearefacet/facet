# Licensing — questions and answers

Facet is **source-available** software. The license is the [Business Source License 1.1](LICENSE) (BSL 1.1) with an Additional Use Grant. The license is loose by intent. It blocks only one thing: reselling Facet itself as a service. Everything else is allowed.

## In short

- You may use Facet, change it, fork it, and build products on it.
- You may sell services around Facet: consulting, development, migration, support, training, and managed hosting for your clients.
- You may not run a self-service Facet offering, and you may not publish one-click Facet images or marketplace listings of domains and/or applications.
- Each release converts to Apache 2.0 four years after its publication.

## Allowed

| Use | Why it is allowed |
| --- | --- |
| Self-hosting Facet for your own organization | Production use by the user is permitted. |
| Consulting, development, and support for clients | The Additional Use Grant permits services to third parties. |
| Hosting and operating Facet for a client under a contract | The grant covers managed hosting within a services agreement. |
| Building apps and domains on Facet, and selling them, including public-facing apps | Your application is your product. The license does not restrict it. |
| Selling domain templates, doctype packs, or extensions that you wrote | Your original work is yours. The license covers Facet, not your creations. |
| Modifying Facet and forking it for your own use | Copy, modify, and redistribute rights are granted to everyone. |
| Internal platforms built on Facet for your staff or customers | The license restricts Facet itself, not what you build on it. |

## Not allowed

- **Self-service Facet offerings.** Any service where third parties sign up and provision Facet instances or tenants on their own — SaaS sign-up, automated provisioning through a portal, API, or marketplace, or published standard plans that anyone can buy without negotiation.
- **Public one-click deployments.** Publishing Facet as pre-packaged images or listings for the general public — for example container or VM images, installers, or cloud marketplace entries.

The test is simple: is Facet itself the product, sold on self-service terms? Then it needs a commercial license from us. Is Facet the tool you use to serve a specific customer under an agreement? Then it is allowed.

## Grey zone? Ask us

Some cases have no clean answer: reselling a product that embeds Facet, a free public demo service, a partnership program, or anything else you are not sure about. Write to us before you launch. We answer fast, and we say yes to fair uses: [contact@amplitude-solutions.com](mailto:contact@amplitude-solutions.com).

## The Change Date

Every Facet release is published under BSL 1.1. Four years after the publication of a release, that release converts to the Apache License, Version 2.0. Older releases stay on Apache 2.0 forever. Newer releases keep their own four-year clock.

## Your data and your work

Facet stores your documents; the license does not claim them. Client-authored domains, doctypes, apps, and data belong to the client who paid for them. Templates, scripts, and extensions you write are your property. The BSL 1.1 license covers the Facet codebase only.

## For contributors

Contributions need a signed Contributor License Agreement — see [CLA.md](CLA.md) and [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). The CLA lets us keep the license consistent and to move releases to Apache 2.0 on the Change Date.
